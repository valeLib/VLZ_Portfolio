A solo Unreal Engine 5 cozy puzzle level where the player prepares a cup of matcha by solving an interconnected sequence of buttons, levers, and moving platforms.

Stack: Unreal Engine 5.7 · Solo project · Universidad Europea de Madrid

Overview

Matcha Puzzle is a single-level 3D puzzle game built around one concept: the player prepares a cup of matcha tea by navigating a multi-tiered isometric environment and activating interactive elements in the correct sequence. No combat, no time pressure, no punishing failure states. The experience rewards observation, spatial reasoning, and patient exploration.

Context: Academic level design project — Universidad Europea de Madrid, 2025–26
Type: Solo project
Engine: Unreal Engine 5.7
Genre: 3D Puzzle / Exploration
Repository: github.com/valeLib/MatchaPuzzle

Design Concept

The central challenge is not combat or timing — it is comprehension. Players must understand which buttons activate which elements, in which order, to reconstruct a logical recipe. The map reveals itself progressively as the player manipulates the environment, opening new routes and reconfiguring platforms.

The thematic coherence between puzzle structure and narrative is intentional: each interactive prop — the matcha tin, kettle, strainer, bamboo whisk, cup — maps directly to a real step in preparing matcha. Progression feels earned and logical rather than arbitrary.

The key design moment is the realization of the correct activation sequence — when the player understands how all the systems connect.

Wrong activation orders require backtracking and reactivation rather than a hard reset. The puzzle has no failure state.

Controls & Camera

  • Camera: Third-person, mouse-orbiting, full horizontal rotation

  • Movement: WASD only — no jump mechanic

  • Vertical navigation: Entirely staircase and drop-based

The no-jump design is a deliberate constraint. Staircases become puzzle resources because the player cannot bypass locked routes. Falling is safe, which encourages spatial exploration without anxiety.

Interactive Elements

Yellow Buttons 1 & 2 and both Blue Buttons operate in paired toggle logic — activating one deactivates the other. This creates a reversible reconfiguration system the player must plan around before acting.

Design Rationale

No jump mechanic — Forces intentional routing. Vertical movement gated by staircases means staircases are puzzle resources, not infrastructure. Players must plan their path rather than improvising.

Paired button logic — Introduces a reversible reconfiguration system the player must reason about before acting. Escalates cognitive load without adding stress or time pressure. Wrong activations aren’t penalized — they require replanning.

No failure state — The cozy genre positioning requires that the emotional register never tip into frustration. Puzzle errors cost time, not progress. The player is always recoverable.

Thematic-mechanical coherence — Every prop and every puzzle step corresponds to a real step in making matcha. When players complete the sequence it feels like they actually made the tea. Narrative and mechanics reinforce each other rather than existing in parallel.
